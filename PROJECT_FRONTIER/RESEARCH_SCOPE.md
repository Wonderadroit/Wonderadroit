# Project Frontier — Research Identity and Scope

**Version:** 0.1  
**Date:** 22 November 2026  
**Status:** Proposed research charter for discussion and independent feedback  
**Repository:** `Wonderadroit/Wonderadroit`

## 1. Identity

**Project Frontier is an independent research and applied-technology initiative examining how trustworthy data, secure digital systems and accountable technology can strengthen migration governance and border management in West Africa.**

It combines documentary and policy research with small, reproducible technical laboratories. It is an independent professional-development and research initiative—not a government agency, immigration service, operational intelligence unit, consultancy claiming government capability, or institution endorsed by NIS, IOM, UNODC, INTERPOL, ICMPD or any university.

## 2. Central research question

**How can data governance, digital identity and cybersecurity practices support trustworthy, rights-respecting and interoperable migration and border-management systems in West Africa?**

This is a programme-level question. Individual studies must narrow it to a defined geography, period, system type, evidence base and method.

## 3. Three connected research themes

### Theme A — Migration data quality and governance

Investigate how migration-related datasets can be made more complete, valid, consistent, timely, traceable and fit for clearly defined analytical purposes.

Methods may include:
- Documentary analysis of public data-governance frameworks.
- Data-contract design and synthetic test datasets.
- Reproducible validation and quality-metric experiments.
- Provenance, duplicate/conflict handling and uncertainty reporting.

Current asset: the synthetic Migration Data Quality Laboratory v0.2. Its tests demonstrate only conformance to a narrow fictional schema; they do not measure the quality of Nigerian or regional operational migration data.

### Theme B — Digital identity and border technology

Investigate identity assurance, biometric and document technologies, interoperability, authentication, privacy, exclusion risks and governance.

Methods may include:
- Structured literature reviews.
- Comparison of public standards and policy documents.
- Threat modelling and architecture analysis.
- Local demonstrations using fictional credentials and test keys only.

Current maturity: a Digital Identity Laboratory design and early verifier exist; successful independent validation remains outstanding. No real identity, passport, NIN, biometric or traveller records are in scope.

### Theme C — Cybersecurity and resilience of migration information systems

Investigate defensive security principles for systems that process sensitive migration-related information.

Methods may include:
- Defensive threat models and security-control mapping.
- Local synthetic authentication and event-detection exercises.
- Secure development, access-control and auditability experiments.
- Reproducible incident-analysis exercises using fabricated logs.

Current asset: a synthetic authentication-detection laboratory with a recorded 17-test CI run. This does not establish production detection effectiveness or operational security capability.

## 4. Geographic and institutional lens

Initial emphasis: Nigeria and the ECOWAS / West African context.

Relevant institutional and normative settings include:
- Nigeria Immigration Service and publicly available Nigerian policy and legal materials.
- ECOWAS free movement and regional border-management frameworks.
- International migration and border-management research by IOM, ICMPD, UNODC and academic institutions.
- Applicable data-protection, privacy, human-rights and cybersecurity standards.

A source's institutional relevance does not imply endorsement, access, partnership or agreement.

## 5. Research method

Each study follows this sequence:

1. Define one answerable question and its scope.
2. Review primary legal, policy and institutional sources first.
3. Review peer-reviewed research and identify competing interpretations.
4. Separate verified facts, authors' arguments, Frontier interpretations and unknowns.
5. Select a method that can answer the question with available evidence.
6. Build a technical laboratory only where an experiment materially tests the question.
7. Record code/data versions, tests, provenance, assumptions and limitations.
8. Seek independent review before presenting the work as a reviewed research contribution.
9. Publish conclusions no stronger than the evidence supports.

Working doctrine: **Sources support. Methods test. Limitations qualify. Humans decide.**

## 6. Explicit exclusions

Frontier will not:
- Collect or process real traveller, passport, NIN, biometric, immigration, personnel or government operational data.
- Access, scan, test or integrate with government or third-party systems without written authorisation covering the exact activity.
- Build person-level migration-risk scores, watchlists, nationality-based suspicion models or automated enforcement recommendations.
- Infer identity, legal status, admissibility or eligibility from a prototype.
- Publish sensitive operational routes, facility details, exploitable weaknesses or security procedures.
- Present synthetic results as evidence of real-world prevalence, system performance or institutional capability.
- Claim official status, institutional affiliation, government endorsement or partnerships that have not been formally established.

Human rights, privacy, due process, non-discrimination and accountable human review are core research constraints, not optional add-ons.

## 7. Initial research agenda

The first three candidate questions are recorded in `RESEARCH_AGENDA.md`. The first technical paper should be based on the existing synthetic Migration Data Quality Laboratory, subject to independent technical and policy review. Do not start another broad platform or dashboard.

## 8. What would make Frontier credible

Credibility will come from:
- Narrow, answerable research questions.
- Accurate use of primary sources and peer-reviewed literature.
- Transparent methods and reproducible experiments.
- Independent review and visible treatment of criticism.
- Clear maturity labels and limitations.
- Useful, accessible writing for both technical and policy audiences.
- A record of ethical, careful and evidence-based work.

The project should be judged by the quality and usefulness of its contributions, not by the number of laboratories, repository commits, institutional names or aspirational claims.

## 9. Current status and review gates

This charter is a proposed scope, not evidence that Frontier has already established a recognised research niche. It should be reviewed against the existing project charter, NIS research brief, legal source register and external academic literature before adoption as the definitive public identity.
