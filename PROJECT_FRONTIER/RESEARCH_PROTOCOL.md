# Research Protocol

## 1. Research question

Every research task must state:
- The exact question.
- Why it matters.
- Geographic and temporal scope.
- What is outside scope.
- What evidence could answer it.
- What would change our conclusion.

## 2. Source hierarchy

Prefer, in order:
1. Official laws, regulations, treaties, strategies, and government publications.
2. Official publications from relevant international and regional organisations.
3. Peer-reviewed research and reputable academic institutions.
4. Reputable independent reporting for context, clearly labelled as secondary.
5. Other sources only when their reliability and limitations are explained.

Do not cite a search-result snippet as if it were the underlying source. Open and inspect the source document.

## 3. Evidence register

For each source, record:
- Title and issuing institution.
- Publication date and access date.
- Stable URL.
- Relevant section/page.
- Claim it supports.
- Whether it is primary or secondary.
- Known limitations or conflicts.

## 4. Analysis discipline

Separate:
- Verified facts.
- Reported claims.
- Interpretations.
- Hypotheses.
- Unknowns.

Do not infer causation from correlation. Do not generalise from one country, border post, or period to an entire region without evidence.

## 5. Privacy and safety

- Use aggregate public data or synthetic records.
- Do not collect, upload, or process real traveller or immigration records.
- Do not attempt to identify individuals from migration datasets.
- Do not create nationality, ethnicity, religion, or other sensitive-trait risk scores.
- Do not test government, border, police, or immigration systems without explicit written authorisation covering the exact activity.
- Do not publish operational vulnerabilities, sensitive configurations, or exploitable details.

## 6. Review checklist

Before publication:
- Are all factual claims traceable?
- Are dates and definitions clear?
- Are limitations explicit?
- Are methods reproducible?
- Is privacy protected?
- Could a reader mistake a prototype for an operational system?
- Does the conclusion stay within what the evidence supports?
