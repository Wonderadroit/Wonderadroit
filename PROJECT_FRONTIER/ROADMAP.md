# Project Frontier Roadmap

This roadmap is adaptive. A phase advances only when its deliverables are evidenced and reviewed.

## Phase 0 — Baseline and scope (Weeks 1–2)

- Inventory current repositories and demonstrable capabilities.
- Establish a skill/evidence matrix.
- Define research ethics, privacy and authorisation boundaries.
- Select a narrow research question.
- Build a primary-source register.

**Exit gate:** approved scope, traceable sources, baseline skills assessment, and written acceptance criteria.

## Phase 1 — Foundations and first research brief (Weeks 3–6)

- Refresh networking, Linux, Python data handling, SQL, and security fundamentals where evidence shows gaps.
- Study identity assurance, authentication, access control, privacy, and threat modelling.
- Review ECOWAS free-movement framework and relevant Nigerian / regional border-management policy from primary sources.
- Produce a concise research brief with citations and limitations.

**Exit gate:** reproducible notes, a reviewed research brief, and a small dataset analysis with provenance.

## Phase 2 — First safe technical prototype (Weeks 7–10)

Build a local-only demonstration using synthetic records. Candidate scope: an identity-verification audit and anomaly-review workflow that records evidence and uncertainty without deciding a person's eligibility or risk.

Possible components:
- Synthetic records generator.
- Input validation and data minimisation.
- Role-based access-control demonstration.
- Audit-event logging.
- Aggregate dashboard.
- Tests for expected and rejected cases.

**Exit gate:** tests, threat model, privacy review, reproducible setup, and explicit limitations.

## Phase 3 — Cybersecurity laboratory (Weeks 11–16)

- Build isolated, defensive labs.
- Practise network traffic interpretation, Linux hardening, access-control testing, logging, and incident-response documentation.
- Publish only sanitised lab reports with no credentials, private data, or unauthorised target details.

**Exit gate:** repeatable lab instructions, test evidence, and remediation notes.

## Phase 4 — Regional research and professional communication (Months 5–8)

- Publish periodic West African migration and border-technology briefs.
- Compare institutional mandates and cooperation mechanisms.
- Produce concise English briefings and presentations.
- Progress French from the current verified level using CEFR-aligned milestones.

**Exit gate:** multiple source-checked publications and reviewed communication samples.

## Phase 5 — Portfolio and opportunity readiness (Months 9–12)

- Consolidate projects, methods, and evidence.
- Map real training, scholarship, secondment, and recruitment opportunities to published eligibility requirements.
- Prepare a factual CV and project portfolio.
- Identify further academic or certification priorities based on demonstrated gaps.

**Exit gate:** independently reviewable portfolio; no unsupported claims of institutional affiliation, operational experience, or deployment eligibility.

## Review cadence

- Weekly: update STATUS.md with completed evidence and blockers.
- Monthly: review the roadmap against actual progress.
- At each phase gate: decide continue, revise, or stop based on evidence and value.

## Resource constraint

Initial work must be Android-friendly and cloud/browser-based where possible. Do not make a laptop purchase a prerequisite for research and planning. Heavy virtualisation and specialist forensic labs can wait until suitable hardware is available.
