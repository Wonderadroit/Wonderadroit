# PROJECT FRONTIER

**Independent professional development and applied-research initiative**  
**Focus:** Migration data governance · Digital identity and border technology · Cybersecurity and resilient information systems

## Mission

Develop and demonstrate practical, evidence-backed capabilities relevant to trustworthy migration data, secure digital identity, technology-enabled border management, and international security cooperation.

Project Frontier is an independent learning and research initiative. It is **not affiliated with, endorsed by, or an official project of the Nigeria Immigration Service, any government agency, INTERPOL, the United Nations, IOM, ICMPD, or another institution**.

## Research identity

**Project Frontier investigates how data governance, digital identity and cybersecurity practices can support trustworthy, rights-respecting and interoperable migration and border-management systems in West Africa.**

The initiative combines documentary and policy research with small, reproducible technical laboratories. Its initial geographic lens is Nigeria and the ECOWAS / West African context.

## Operating principles

1. **Evidence before claims.** Every technical or research conclusion must have a traceable source, method and limitation.
2. **Defensive and authorised work only.** Security labs use local, synthetic or explicitly authorised environments.
3. **Privacy by design.** No real traveller, passport, NIN, biometric, immigration, personnel or government operational data.
4. **Human accountability.** Analytical outputs support human review; they do not make immigration, law-enforcement or eligibility decisions.
5. **No operational profiling.** No individual risk scores, watchlists, nationality-based suspicion or automated adverse decisions.
6. **Research before implementation.** Establish the problem and evidence before building a tool.
7. **Honest maturity labels.** Distinguish learning exercise, prototype, validated result and production system.
